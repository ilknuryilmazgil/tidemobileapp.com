How the 90/180 rule works

  • Visitors who do not need a Schengen visa, and those with a short-stay visa, can stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period across all Schengen countries together.
  • Every day counts on its own: on each day of your stay, the days spent in the previous 180 days, including that day, must not exceed 90.
  • Both the entry day and the exit day count as days of stay.
  • Ireland and Cyprus are not in the Schengen Area; days there are counted separately.

Europe now counts your days automatically

Since 10 April 2026, the EU Entry/Exit System (EES) registers non-EU nationals travelling for a short stay. It records your travel document, fingerprints, facial image and the date and place of each entry and exit.

The EES replaces passport stamps and allows the automatic detection of travellers who stay longer than allowed. Knowing your own day count before you travel matters more than ever.

Examples

One long trip

You stayed from 1 March to 29 April 2026: 60 days, counting both the entry and exit day. If you enter again on 1 June 2026, you can stay up to 30 more days.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a separate 90-day rule for Spain, France or Italy?

No. Days in all Schengen countries are added together into one 90/180 count. Some countries have older bilateral agreements with certain nationalities; check with that country’s authorities if you rely on one.

Does the rule apply if I have a Schengen visa?

Yes. A short-stay visa also follows the 90/180 rule, and you must stay within the dates and number of days printed on the visa.

What happens if I overstay?

Overstaying can lead to penalties decided by the border authorities, which vary by country. With the EES, overstays are detected automatically.

Is there an official calculator?

Yes. The European Commission offers a short-stay calculator. This page follows the same rule and gives you an estimate.
