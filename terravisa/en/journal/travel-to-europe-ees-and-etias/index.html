A New Era at Europe’s Borders: EES and ETIAS

How the Entry/Exit System (EES) that replaces passport stamps works, who ETIAS will cover and when it will start, summarised from official EU sources.

November 11, 20265 min readLast checked: November 12, 2026

Open your passport and leaf through the pages. Every stamp is the trace of a border, an arrival, a journey. In Europe, these small marks of ink are gradually giving way to something else: a photo of your face, your fingerprints and the digital record that holds them.

And the change doesn’t stop there. On the horizon is a brand-new travel authorisation for visa-exempt travellers. In this article we explain both systems, staying faithful to what the EU’s official “Travel to Europe” website says.

The system replacing the stamp: EES

The Entry/Exit System (EES) is an automated IT system that registers non-EU nationals travelling for a short stay each time they cross the external borders of the European countries using it.

So where will you meet it? In the 29 countries using the system: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland.

It covers both groups: travellers who need a visa for a short stay in these countries, and those who are visa-exempt. A “short stay” here means up to 90 days in any 180-day period, calculated as a single period for all the countries using the system.

Did you know? Cyprus and Ireland are EU members but do not use the EES. In these two countries, passports are still stamped by hand, just as before.

What awaits you at the border?

You join the queue, and it’s your turn. What happens next depends on whether this is your first meeting with the system or you are already acquainted.

The first time you cross after the EES has started, you will have to provide your personal data. Passport control officers will take a photo of your face and/or scan your fingerprints, and this information is recorded in a digital file. Your passport, biometric or not, will not be stamped.

There are ways to speed up this first step. You can register some of your data in advance:

  • using self-service equipment, if available at your border crossing point,
  • using a mobile app, if the country of arrival or departure provides one.

Either way, you will meet a passport control officer.

On later crossings, things get easier. Your facial image and/or fingerprints will already be in the system, so officers only verify them, which takes less time. If you hold a biometric passport and self-service equipment is available, your crossing can be even quicker.

The EU describes the system’s aims as making border checks faster and more modern, preventing overstays and improving security.

What comes next? ETIAS

ETIAS is a new travel authorisation that visa-exempt travellers will need to enter 30 European countries. Here is what is known for certain today, straight from the official EU website:

  • ETIAS is not yet in operation, and no applications are being collected.
  • The EU will announce the start date several months before it launches.
  • The application fee will be EUR 20.
  • An ETIAS authorisation will allow stays of up to 90 days.

One detail matters: ETIAS is for visa-exempt travellers. If your passport requires a Schengen visa, your route is a visa application, and we walk through it step by step in our Schengen visa article.

No ETIAS applications are being accepted yet. Follow updates only on the EU’s official “Travel to Europe” website, and be wary of unofficial websites claiming to accept applications.

Europe’s borders are changing, but for travellers who set off well informed, the journey stays just as enjoyable. Curious how you can enter each country with your passport? Our country pages are just a click away.
